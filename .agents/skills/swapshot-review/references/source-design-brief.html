01

The game, in one minute

One operative. One facility. One ability that turns bad positioning into an advantage.

You are Vex, an operative trapped inside the Helix research facility after a security breach. To extract its experimental core, fight through guards and automated defenses using an unstable SHIFT Device. Plant an anchor, lure enemies into a bad angle, and instantly appear somewhere they didn't expect.

01 / Engage

Take the fight

Third-person over-the-shoulder shooting. Move quickly, use cover, and identify threats.

02 / Outthink

Place an anchor

Aim at a reachable floor or elevated surface and deploy a holographic destination.

03 / Dominate

SHIFT and flank

Teleport, leave a decoy behind, and turn the new angle into a decisive attack.

The one-line design rule: The shooting must feel good on its own; SHIFT should make a good firefight spectacular.

Player / equipment

Keep combat focused

One VX-7 rifle: automatic hitscan, 24-round magazine, ~1.5-second reload, readable recoil, tracers, hit markers, and strong sound.

Two enemy archetypes: security troopers who fire and reposition; an optional stationary turret with a telegraphed firing arc.

Fast retries: 100 HP, regenerate after a short safe interval, checkpoint per zone. No crafting, loot economy, or gear system.

03

One mission. Three escalating zones.

A compact campaign with an intro, combat escalation, a clear objective, and a replayable conclusion.

ZONE 01

Loading Dock

2–3 min · Learn the basics

Enter the Helix compound, learn rifle handling, deploy your first SHIFT anchor, and flank two guards behind stacked cargo.

ZONE 02

Security Core

3–4 min · Vertical firefight

Fight across cover and catwalks, use high ground, avoid security defenses, and disable the control terminal.

Take the experimental core, hold against the last enemy wave, then escape. Replay to improve your performance.

04

How we'd build it

Simple enough to ship as one developer. Modular enough to maintain. No servers, accounts, or multiplayer.

Recommended engine

Godot 4.7.2 + GDScript

Use CharacterBody3D for movement, SpringArm3D for the third-person camera, raycasts for hitscan weapons and placement checks, and AnimationPlayer for effects. Export with the Compatibility renderer and single-threaded web settings.

Ship first, optimize second

Web playable, desktop optional

Publish an HTML5 build on itch.io, test on normal laptop browsers, and link a public GitHub repository. Keep geometry, effects, and download size modest; verify the hosted game, not only the editor build.

Technical design: the safe SHIFT sequence
  1. Place one anchor using range and line-of-sight checks.
  2. Validate a floor and enough empty space for the player's collision capsule.
  3. On SHIFT, recheck clearance because the world could have changed.
  4. Teleport to the anchor; preserve camera aim and safely adjust velocity.
  5. Spawn a 1.5-second holographic echo at the old position; notify nearby enemies.
  6. Consume the anchor, start cooldown, and update the HUD.

If placement or activation fails validation, do not consume the ability. Never teleport through solid geometry.

Technical design: enemy and weapon behaviour

Enemy states: Idle → Alerted → Attacking / Repositioning → Distracted → Dead. Start with cover points rather than complicated squad AI. Keep a small number of enemies active at once.

Rifle: Camera-based aiming, muzzle obstruction checks, rate limiting, finite magazine, short reload, hit reactions and sound. Don't allow bullets to pass through nearby walls.

Highest technical risks: clumsy third-person controls, unsafe teleports, mediocre shooting feedback, heavy browser rendering, and spending too long on 3D assets. Prototype in primitive shapes before investing in art.

Optional reference: GDQuest third-person controller demo. Evaluate compatibility and its license before reusing code or assets. Kenney Prototype Kit can speed up greyboxing.

05

The first 48 hours

Build the smallest thing that proves the game will be fun. No polished environments. No cinematic intro. Just one fight.

Day 1 · Nov 2

Movement + SHIFT

~5–6 focused hours

  • Create Godot project and small arena.
  • Get third-person movement and camera working.
  • Add rifle firing and damageable target.
  • Place an anchor and teleport to it.
  • Test camera behaviour and collisions.
Day 2 · Nov 3

One full firefight

~5–6 focused hours

  • Add one enemy that detects and shoots.
  • Add health, death, and restart.
  • Add anchor cooldown and holographic echo.
  • Add muzzle flash, hit feedback, SHIFT sound.
  • Export and test the first browser build.
Go / no-go question: Can a player move, shoot, place an anchor, SHIFT into a clever flanking position, defeat an enemy, and immediately want another attempt? If not, tune the fundamentals before building levels.

07

What actually ships

Set a hard quality bar and cut extras before the game becomes too large to finish.

P0 Must ship

One compact arena, one rifle, one enemy type, reliable SHIFT, health/restart, winning condition, essential feedback, a playable browser build.

P1 Planned release

Three connected zones, terminal objective, checkpoints, optional turret, polished effects and sound, extraction finale, replay score.

P2 Only if ahead

More animations, controller support, challenge mode, extra encounters, alternate desktop builds. No multiplayer, open world, or weapon customization.

Release quality gates

GameplayThird-person movement, aiming, recoil, and SHIFT all feel responsive.
Safety of gameplayNo wall-teleports, softlocks, uncontrolled respawns, or shots through solid cover.

UsabilityClear SHIFT status, controls, pause, restart, and accessible contrast.
